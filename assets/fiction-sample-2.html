Best of All Possible Burritos

Fiction • By Adam Krieger • 2026

In nineteenth-century America…there was a social convention called the "barbecue law," which meant that all the men who gathered for a barbecue were expected to drink until they were soused. To refuse a drink entailed a serious insult to the host and the rest of the party.

— Willpower: Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength

The dentist sat quietly waiting for the drugs to kick in before he began to irrigate the gums. His patient sat, waiting in the highchair as a menagerie of high pitched squeals and fumes of gas began to till the air...

I woke up barefoot in a stupor on the middle of the railroad tracks. My olive oil surplus jacket laid beneath me with my shoes pinch hitting as a pillow. The sky was burning my face and I couldn't wait to shed my skin.

I wasn't there for too long, the trains run early every morning. I'd be dead by now if this wasn't hell already.

"That could have ended badly. Must be my lucky day!"

If there was ever a time to celebrate it was then. Abbey's Cafe it was. I checked my pockets. Phone, wallet, $20, five-pound piece of steel rebar, peppermint Binaca spray, and a Zippo lighter inside an empty package of baloney.

"A bunch of useless stuff. I think that's everything."

Fuck, it was Christmas! Merry goddamn new year I guess. I could tell I'd been drinking expensive whiskey because my breath smelled like fermented cheese and honey-bacon.

It was a dog day for sure. My throat was so dry I couldn't swallow so I used the Binaca. That shit is 43% alcohol it just made things worse. I had a 10-mile walk ahead of me. I flung my jacket around my shoulders like a cape and flew away.

Albeit not quite real flying, I was too weak to do anything but hover slightly above sea level, with my head bobbing up and down like counterweight.

The Perfect Bite

My perfect burrito is a chorizo and egg. I couldn't stop thinking about opening my present and sinking my gums into those spicy, juicy clouds of fluff wrapped up in a burnt up buttery blanket. Softly beaten childhood memories groaned deep inside my belly. My stomach growled like a baby in a bottle...

"Man, I should've worn shorts."

"Only bashful people don't wear shorts," is what my pet bear would say whenever we met in the woods. "You need meat on your booones," which was true enough to make me angry...

As I sat still, I began to puke. Bits and pieces of the night came back to me. "Oh god no!" I couldn't even look at my phone, who knows what the hell I said or did. What if I got stoned and ate a bird or something? I swore off meat until Lent was over, but I had no idea what year it was, let alone which season.

I switched my phone on. "Heyyyy, half charged, nice." Things weren't so bad after all; I could fondle this thing for another three hours. I vigorously swiped up and down until I came to a conclusion and my head was clear.

Eyes Kant See It

"I'm going to miss this dumpster. Why didn't I sleep here last night? The Best of All Possible dumpsters." I pretended I was in the mountains where I used to stay. I'd shoegaze during the day and stargaze naked underneath a free bed of leaves at night. Then wake up soaking wet in the middle of the night. The sweet smell of maple leaves and cherry alder wood soaking into my pores. Breidablik... home of my lord Balder. Nordic Eden.

I left because Eden was boring. So I took off to wander the desert with my Eve. We were so bored we had children. Then I got so bored I began searching for the best burritos in the desert. Chorizo and eggs are certainly not boring. I could eat them for the rest of my life.

The birds had seemed to calm down, I figured now was my chance. I took out my lighter and the Binaca. Then stood up slowly, raising the hood with the crown of my skull. Like flies buzzing around a piece of shit, they hovered near the open lid.

I sparked my zippo and began to pop up and down like a jack in the box, pumping the Binaca at the lit flame until it exploded like a mini flamethrower. I did this over and over until I was out of fuel, then quickly abandoned that plan. All it did was burn the hair on my knuckles, which I lapped up like cotton candy.

I popped up again and slammed the dumpster open and threw the chili fries as far away as I could. The whole dozen of them fought over the fries. I didn't stick the landing; landing hard on my right side and screamed out, "That fucking chain!" The rebar rolled under the dumpster to the magnets on the wall.

I looked up and realized I was fucked. There were 40 or 50 of them lined up on both sides of the alley rooftops, ready to pounce. "Cawk, Cawk, Cawk!" They were nagging me from all sides with a New Jersey nanny housewife accent. I was in a circle of shame.

I wrapped the chain around my neck and made my move. I swung my jacket around wildly as they swarmed and ran towards Abbey's...

Everything moved in slow motion...

Author’s Note

This is a mix of original content with some narrative driven fiction from my favorite sci-fi and pulp novel stories from authors like Philip K. Dick, John Fante, and Harlan Ellison. Beyond these pulp influences, the narrative draws heavily upon the classic "best of all possible worlds" philosophical trope popularized by Voltaire's Candide, juxtaposing absurd optimism against stark, sun-baked grit. Complete with Easter egg quotes from some of my favorite artists and thinkers.
