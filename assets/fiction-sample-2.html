Best of All Possible Burritos

Fiction • By Adam Krieger • 2026

In nineteenth-century America…there was a social convention called the "barbecue law," which meant that all the men who gathered for a barbecue were expected to drink until they were soused. To refuse a drink entailed a serious insult to the host and the rest of the party.
— Willpower: Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength

I woke up barefoot in a stupor on the middle of the train tracks. My olive oil surplus jacket was underneath me and my shoes were pinch-hitting as a pillow. The sky was burning my face I couldn’t wait to shed my skin...

The Perfect Bite

My perfect burrito is chorizo and egg. I couldn't stop thinking about opening my present. Sinking my gums into those spicy, juicy clouds wrapped in a buttery blanket. Softly beaten childhood memories swished deep inside my body.

"Man, I should've worn shorts."

"Only bashful people don't wear shorts," – my wife's obese aunt. "You need meat on your bones." Maybe I should bash her face in and eat her. I could live for years on that sow. I could get wasted off her blood. She was a nurse who was addicted to the drugs at her job. Even after she got fired for stealing pills and went to rehab, her insurance covered an implanted Dilaudid pump and a slew of mind-altering drugs.

Due for a heart attack any minute. I couldn't wait till the coca-cola and sandwiches strangled her arteries. WTF was I thinking? This shit doesn't pop into my mind when I'm not hungry.

She was terrible to everybody. I wasn't sure if she really bothered me or I had contracted diabetes. One thing was sure, Abbey's Cafe was open on holidays and they had the best breakfast in town. There were still a lot of elderly folks living in old folks' homes that eat there every Christmas.

The polyester sun was golden brown. It was always dry and hot in that hot in the arena of surburban sprawl, 180 miles south of LA...

Oriental Dungeon

This is how I'm going to die? Suffocate to death in a gas chamber. The stench of death and used mountain fresh dryer sheets mixed with my own breath was going to slowly gas me out. The place was less than half full but I was comfortable squatting on the trash bags and old unwanted clothing.

Ropes of light were shining in from outside. It was kind of beautiful. I felt special. A crack in the sky and a hand reaching down to me…austere beauty. I was doomed, thirsty for endless pleasure. An aesthetic without faith or morals. "This isn't so bad, why didn't I sleep in here?"

I ripped open a black trash bag and opened a styrofoam container. "Chili cheese fries? Don't mind if I do." I ate a couple but I was queasy, didn't want to push my luck. The rest were my ticket outta there, birds love french fries.

One of the light beams illuminated something shiny. It was a windchime with an inscription on it…

In his activities, he loves timeliness
It is because he does not compete that he is without reproach
— Lao-Tzu Chinese Philosopher
