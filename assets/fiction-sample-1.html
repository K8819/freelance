Yoni and the Lingam

By ArtFarmer • Fiction, Texts • 2026

She made a wish, closed her eyes and leaned in for a kiss, his body recoiled with pain and pleasure before he stuck her neck with his fangs…

“You brought me back to life? You bitch!”

“You shot all your venom in me asshole, I’m probably going to have to go to the hospital. Are you venomous?”

“I was a baby again, that’s what we do, but I can’t even do that now. My venom was poisonous but not to you, you will get sick, perhaps even forever, but you will be okay.”

Her head began to swim and her eyes started to glow, she felt like she was skipping down the street on Sunday morning. She gently stroked his long dark wing as he purred and ruffled his feathers.

“I’m sorry, you looked so beautiful lying there in the tall grass. But you are even cuter now, plus I gave you the gift of gab. I didn’t think a kiss would, well…do that.”

“It was your wish, and you made me into a bird? You could’ve made me into a handsome prince.”

“Would you rather be a tree? Unaware of your own existence, stuck in one spot forever? Besides, I’d rather have a talking bird than a handsome prince.”

“That’s what I was hoping for, not this mortal coil, what do I even eat? And what is your name?”

“My name, my name is metaphysics. And you can eat whatever you want. The town is your oyster.”

“Cute, but what should I call you?”

“My name is Guinevere, my friends call me Joni.”

“Well nice to meet you Guinevere. I’ll call you Yoni, with a soft J.”

“And you?”

“I’ve never had a name, but you can call me…Lingam.”

“Lovely to meet you Lingam,” she said, with little hearts dancing in her eyes, “But I have to go now, I feel, strange.”

She took off her clothes and disappeared into the scummy brown, brackish pond water. “Goodbye my love.”

The Morning After

There wasn’t much time. It had to be done before the noon day sun reached it’s apex. She reached the bottom of a deep ravine carpeted with snow white sand, saw a prickly pair cactus, closed her eyes and it began to speak.

After the communion, she collected herself, started a fire and opened her bag. White snake, ghost-rib cactus, and fine black obsidian sand went into the mix. She slowly crushed the cactus pulp into a thick, sticky green paste and bound it with powdered snake scales as it bubbled up into a sea of foam.

She made a wish, leaned in and blew out the candle, something she’d do each night before bed. She took the burning hot black sand and added to the concoction.

Soon after she ingested it, she became violently ill and fell asleep. Her naked, shivering body recoiled in horror. Fever dreams were full of unholy machines, inhumanity, and the voices of one thousand tiny sons and moons crying out in pain.

When she woke up, she felt better, but she knew what she had done. She could no longer speak to animals, she could no longer understand their needs, but she was queen of herself, whole again. And she forgot all about Lingam and the snake, the crow and his song, and the language of the sun and moon.

Author’s Note

This piece was conceived as an exercise within the supernatural romance genre, operating very much in the stylistic vein of Cleopatra and Frankenstein by Coco Mellors and Mary Shelley’s Frankenstein meets Christopher Moore’s You Suck. As Shelley famously reflected on the haunting power of romantic obsession and creation, fiction must touch upon those forces that "wakened thrilling horror—one should be cognizant of the unseen powers that rule our existence."

More specifically, this narrative functions as a direct inversion of the opening premise of Christopher Moore’s You Suck. By twisting those initial beats from Moore's contemporary vampire universe inside out.
